Mental Health Awareness — Stress, Anxiety, and Depression
Source: WHO Mental Health Resources / General Educational Content

STRESS
Stress is the body's natural response to challenges or demands. Short-term stress can be helpful,
but chronic stress can negatively affect physical and mental health.

Common symptoms of stress:
- Difficulty concentrating or making decisions
- Feeling overwhelmed or out of control
- Irritability, mood changes
- Physical symptoms: headaches, muscle tension, fatigue, sleep disturbance
- Social withdrawal

Healthy ways to manage stress:
- Regular physical activity (even a short walk helps)
- Adequate sleep (7–9 hours for adults)
- Mindfulness or deep-breathing exercises
- Talking to a trusted friend, family member, or professional
- Balanced nutrition and limiting caffeine/alcohol
- Setting realistic goals and learning to say no
- Taking breaks and scheduling enjoyable activities

ANXIETY
Anxiety is a normal emotion, but anxiety disorders involve excessive fear or worry that interferes
with daily activities. Anxiety disorders are among the most common mental health conditions.

Types include: Generalised Anxiety Disorder (GAD), Social Anxiety, Panic Disorder, Phobias.

Common symptoms:
- Persistent worry that is difficult to control
- Restlessness or feeling on edge
- Fatigue, concentration difficulties
- Muscle tension
- Sleep problems
- Physical symptoms: rapid heartbeat, shortness of breath, sweating

Coping strategies for anxiety:
- Grounding techniques (5-4-3-2-1 sensory method)
- Controlled breathing: inhale 4 seconds, hold 4 seconds, exhale 6 seconds
- Challenging anxious thoughts (cognitive reframing)
- Limiting news and social media consumption
- Regular physical activity and sleep
- Seeking professional support (therapy, counseling)

DEPRESSION
Depression (major depressive disorder) is a serious but treatable condition characterised
by persistent low mood, loss of interest, and reduced energy.

Common symptoms:
- Persistent sadness, emptiness, or hopelessness
- Loss of interest in activities previously enjoyed
- Changes in appetite or weight
- Sleep disturbances (sleeping too much or too little)
- Fatigue and reduced energy
- Difficulty concentrating, remembering, or deciding
- Feelings of worthlessness or excessive guilt
- In severe cases, thoughts of death or suicide

Depression is NOT a sign of weakness. It is a medical condition that responds to treatment.

Treatment options:
- Psychotherapy (CBT, talk therapy)
- Medication (as prescribed by a qualified professional)
- Lifestyle changes: exercise, sleep, nutrition, social connection
- Support groups

IMPORTANT: If you or someone you know is experiencing symptoms of depression, please
consult a qualified mental health professional. Early intervention leads to better outcomes.

WHEN TO SEEK HELP
You should consider speaking with a mental-health professional if:
- Symptoms persist for more than two weeks
- Symptoms interfere with daily functioning
- You are having thoughts of harming yourself or others
- Self-help strategies are not working

Remember: seeking help is a sign of strength, not weakness.
