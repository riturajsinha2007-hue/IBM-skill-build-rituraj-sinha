MINDFULNESS
Mindfulness is the practice of paying attention to the present moment without judgment.
It can reduce stress, anxiety, and depressive symptoms.

Simple mindfulness practices:
1. Mindful breathing: Focus on each breath in and out. When thoughts arise, gently return
   attention to the breath.
2. Body scan: Slowly bring awareness to each part of the body, noticing sensations without judgment.
3. Mindful walking: Focus on the sensations of walking — the feel of the ground, rhythm of steps.
4. Gratitude journaling: Write three things you are grateful for each day.
5. Mindful eating: Eat slowly, savoring flavours and textures.

DEEP BREATHING TECHNIQUES

Box Breathing (used by military, first responders):
- Inhale for 4 seconds
- Hold for 4 seconds
- Exhale for 4 seconds
- Hold for 4 seconds
- Repeat 4–5 times

4-7-8 Breathing:
- Inhale through the nose for 4 seconds
- Hold for 7 seconds
- Exhale slowly through the mouth for 8 seconds

GROUNDING TECHNIQUES (for anxiety and panic)

5-4-3-2-1 Method:
- Name 5 things you can see
- Name 4 things you can touch
- Name 3 things you can hear
- Name 2 things you can smell
- Name 1 thing you can taste

COGNITIVE REFRAMING
- Identify the anxious or negative thought
- Ask: "Is this thought based on facts or fears?"
- Look for evidence for and against the thought
- Generate a more balanced, realistic perspective

PHYSICAL SELF-CARE
Regular physical activity has strong evidence for improving mental health:
- Even 20–30 minutes of walking per day can reduce symptoms of depression and anxiety
- Exercise releases endorphins that improve mood
- Yoga and stretching reduce muscle tension from stress

Sleep hygiene:
- Maintain a consistent sleep schedule
- Avoid screens one hour before bed
- Keep the bedroom dark and cool
- Avoid caffeine after 2pm

SOCIAL SUPPORT
Human connection is one of the most powerful protective factors for mental health.
- Reach out to a trusted friend or family member when you are struggling
- Consider joining a support group
- Professional counselors and therapists are trained to help

JOURNALING FOR MENTAL HEALTH
Regular journaling can:
- Help process difficult emotions
- Identify patterns in mood and thought
- Provide a safe private outlet for feelings
- Track progress in recovery or wellbeing

Tips for effective journaling:
- Write without judgment — there is no wrong way
- Focus on feelings, not just events
- Review entries periodically to notice patterns
- Consider sharing with a therapist for deeper insight
