Crisis Support and Suicide Prevention — Educational Resource
Source: WHO Suicide Prevention / Verified crisis organisations

UNDERSTANDING SUICIDAL THOUGHTS
Suicidal thoughts (suicidal ideation) exist on a spectrum — from passive thoughts of not
wanting to be alive, to active plans. They are a sign of extreme pain, NOT a character flaw.

Most people who experience suicidal thoughts do NOT act on them, especially with support.

WARNING SIGNS TO WATCH FOR IN YOURSELF OR OTHERS:
- Talking about wanting to die or kill oneself
- Looking for ways to kill oneself (searching online, acquiring means)
- Talking about being a burden to others
- Expressing feelings of hopelessness or having no reason to live
- Withdrawing from friends, family, activities
- Giving away prized possessions
- Saying goodbye as if not expecting to see people again
- Extreme mood changes — sudden calmness after deep depression can be a warning sign
- Increased substance use
- Reckless or self-destructive behaviour

PROTECTIVE FACTORS (things that reduce risk):
- Strong social support and connection
- Access to mental health care
- Reasons for living (family, pets, goals, beliefs)
- Problem-solving and coping skills
- Restricted access to lethal means

HOW TO HELP SOMEONE IN CRISIS:
1. Ask directly — research shows asking does not "plant the idea"
   "Are you having thoughts of suicide?" or "Are you thinking about harming yourself?"
2. Listen without judgment — do not try to argue or minimise feelings
3. Stay with them — do not leave someone alone if you believe they are in immediate danger
4. Remove access to lethal means where possible
5. Help connect them to professional support
6. Follow up after the crisis

WHAT NOT TO SAY:
- Do not say: "You have so much to live for" (can feel invalidating)
- Do not say: "Others have it worse" (minimises pain)
- Do not challenge or dare someone who is suicidal
- Do not promise to keep suicidal thoughts secret

IMMEDIATE ACTION IF SOMEONE IS IN DANGER:
- Call emergency services (local emergency number: 112 in India, 911 in USA, 999 in UK)
- Take the person to the nearest hospital emergency room
- Contact a crisis helpline for guidance

RECOVERY IS POSSIBLE
The vast majority of people who survive a suicidal crisis go on to live full lives.
Effective treatments exist. Reaching out for help is the most important first step.
